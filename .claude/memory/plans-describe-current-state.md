---
name: plans-describe-current-state
description: Write plans as if they had always read this way — no references to earlier iterations, and justify decisions against the current plan only
metadata:
  type: feedback
---

When writing or revising a plan, never refer to its previous state. No "changed from", "previously we said", "instead of the earlier approach", no revision notes. Justifications for why the plan is the way it is must be made with respect to the *current* plan, not an earlier iteration of it.

**Why:** A reader arriving fresh has no idea what the old version said, so references to it are noise at best and misleading at worst — the plan reads as confusing rather than decided.

**How to apply:** After editing a plan, reread it as if seeing it for the first time and strip anything that only makes sense to someone who read the prior draft. Keep the reasoning, drop the diff. Same discipline as the articles rule in [[docs-plans-vs-articles]] — the plan describes what is intended now, not how the intent evolved.
